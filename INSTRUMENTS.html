Anatomy of an Instrument

Four drawings of the same idea: what the unit is, where the wall sits, what makes a thing worth keeping on screen, and how it earns its place twice.

01 — THE UNIT

An instrument is a question, not a place

The first version of this design made the surface the package. That turns one capability into five unrelated things that have to be kept in step by hand.

02 — THE BOUNDARY

The wall, and the two doors in it

This is the drawing that answers “how does an HTML thing fetch data?” It doesn’t. Everything that crosses the wall goes through a door bob owns; everything inside the projection is the instrument’s own business.

bob owns 100% of the outer grammar — effects, focus, failure, navigation — and 20–40% of the pixels.

03 — RELEVANCE

An address is not a moment

The usual way extensibility dies is supply without recurring demand: a thing exists and nothing ever brings it back. Living somewhere permanent doesn’t fix that — it just spends attention instead of clicks.

04 — LIFECYCLE

Made in a second, kept on purpose

If asking produces an instrument in ten seconds, the collection gets worse, not better — nothing forces the discipline that difficulty used to. So creation is free and permanence is what costs something.
